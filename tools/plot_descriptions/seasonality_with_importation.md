**Reading the seasonality-plus-importation plot.**
Same two-axis SIR layout — 10 years on x, S/R on the left axis (0 to 1e6), I on the right axis (0 to ~14,000). The gray sinusoidal seasonality curve is identical to the previous figure (annual cycle, $\pm 20\%$ around 1.0, anchored to day 214).

The red I trajectory now shows a sustained, regular biennial pattern: large sharp peaks near days ~1,000, ~1,800, ~2,500, and ~3,250 — spaced almost exactly 730 days apart — reaching ~13,000–14,000 agents infectious. Between the major peaks the I curve hugs zero with only small ripples, and one small early outbreak near day ~400 reaches ~7,000. Each major peak is timed to coincide with a seasonality maximum, and transmission attenuates sharply on the descending half of the sinusoid. S rebuilds linearly to ~85,000 between peaks before being depleted again; R sits near 950,000 with shallow saw-tooth dips coincident with each outbreak.

**The plot demonstrates that constant low-rate importation (3 cases every 30 days) is enough to break the fadeout problem and let seasonal forcing entrain a stable biennial outbreak cycle phase-locked to the annual $\beta(t)$ peak.**
