#### Reading the sinusoidal-forcing (no importation) plot

Same axes layout as the baseline plot — 10 years on x, S/R on the left axis (0 to 1e6), I on the right axis (0 to ~8,500). The gray "Seasonality (scaled)" curve is now a clean sinusoid completing 10 cycles, oscillating $\pm 20\%$ around 1.0 with peaks anchored to day 214 + multiples of 365 (start of school). This is the $1 + 0.2\sin((t-214) \cdot 2\pi/365)$ forcing.

Three sharp red I peaks appear near days ~50, ~400, and ~1,050, growing in amplitude from ~2,700 to ~8,500. After the third peak the infectious count crashes to zero and stays there — fadeout / local elimination. The blue S curve drifts steadily upward from ~50,000 to ~240,000 after extinction (births accumulating with no transmission to consume them), and R decays linearly from ~960,000 toward ~760,000 as recovered agents are recycled out.

**The plot demonstrates that seasonal forcing of $\beta(t)$ synchronizes outbreaks into larger, sharper annual peaks, but in a closed population the deeper troughs drive the infection extinct — motivating the importation experiment that follows.**
