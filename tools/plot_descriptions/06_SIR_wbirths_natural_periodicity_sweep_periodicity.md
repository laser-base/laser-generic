### Reading the parameter-sweep periodicity plots

Ten figures, one per simulation in the $(\mu, \gamma, R_0)$ sweep (`nsims=10`, CBR sampled in 70–100, `inf_mean` in 50–100, $R_0$ in 5–15). Each panel shares the same anatomy as the single-run sanity-check plot above: blue mean-subtracted, Gaussian-smoothed infected trajectory $I_t$ versus tick index over the ~50-year post-cutoff window (x-axis $\sim$0–18,250 days, y-axis spanning roughly $\pm 300$–$500$), with red `x` markers at peaks and black `x` markers at valleys, and a top-right text block reporting `T expected`, peak-finder, valley-finder, and FFT-based observed periods in years.

Across the ten panels, $T_{\text{exp}}$ ranges from 2.40 y (sim 0) to 4.66 y (sim 2). The three estimators are individually noisy — for sim 0, peak-finder and valley-finder give 2.01 y and 2.19 y against $T_{\text{exp}} = 2.40$ y, while FFT lands at 1.15 y (a classic second-harmonic landing on $T_{\text{exp}}/2$); for sim 2, all three (3.59, 3.45, 4.20 y) bracket $T_{\text{exp}} = 4.66$ y from below and above. The longer-period sims (sims 2, 6 at $T_{\text{exp}} \approx 4.3$–4.7 y) show fewer, broader oscillations across the window; the shorter-period sims (sims 0, 4, 9 at $T_{\text{exp}} \approx 2.4$–2.6 y) pack noticeably more cycles into the same axis.

**The figures demonstrate that the analytic natural-period formula $T \approx 2\pi\sqrt{A G}$ tracks the observed oscillation period of the stochastic SIR-with-births model across a sweep of $(\mu, \gamma, R_0)$ tuples**, with method-specific estimators (peak-finder, valley-finder, autocorrelation FFT) noisy enough individually that the downstream programmatic validation takes a best-of-three per simulation.
