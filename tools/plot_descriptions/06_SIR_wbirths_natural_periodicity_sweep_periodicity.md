### Reading the parameter-sweep periodicity plots

Five figures, one per simulation in the $(\mu, \gamma, R_0)$ sweep (`nsims=5`, seeded `np.random.seed(42)`, CBR sampled in 70–100, `inf_mean` in 50–100, $R_0$ in 5–15). Each panel shares the same anatomy as the single-run sanity-check plot above: blue mean-subtracted, Gaussian-smoothed infected trajectory $I_t$ versus tick index over the post-cutoff window (10-year transient dropped from a 40-year simulation), with red `x` markers at peaks and black `x` markers at valleys, and a top-right text block reporting `T expected` alongside peak-finder, valley-finder, and FFT-based observed periods in years.

Across the five panels, $T_{\text{exp}}$ typically ranges over roughly 2–5 years. The three estimators are individually noisy — the peak-finder and valley-finder can drift on noisy signals, and the FFT autocorrelation is known to land on $T_{\text{exp}}/2$ when the signal carries a strong second harmonic — so any single method may fall well off the diagonal on any given sim. The next cell's scatter plot (observed vs analytic) shows the three overlaid; taken together they bracket $T_{\text{exp}}$.

**The figures demonstrate that the analytic natural-period formula $T \approx 2\pi\sqrt{A G}$ tracks the observed oscillation period of the stochastic SIR-with-births model across a sweep of $(\mu, \gamma, R_0)$ tuples**, with method-specific estimators (peak-finder, valley-finder, autocorrelation FFT) noisy enough individually that the downstream programmatic validation takes a best-of-three per simulation. A full-fidelity 10-sim, 100-year version of this sweep is kept at `docs/tutorials/notebooks/research/06_SIR_wbirths_natural_periodicity.ipynb` for research use.
