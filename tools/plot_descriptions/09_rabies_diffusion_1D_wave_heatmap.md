### Reading the spatial-spread heatmap

A two-dimensional intensity map of the infectious compartment $I$ over the full simulation: x-axis is time in ticks (0 to ~730), y-axis is patch index along the 1D lattice (0 to 200), and the viridis colorbar runs from 0 (deep purple) to ~675 infectives per patch (bright yellow). The simulation seeds three infectives at the central patch (index 101), and from that origin two narrow bright-yellow stripes fan outward in a near-perfect ">" shape — one rising upward toward patch 200, one descending toward patch 0 — both reaching the boundaries at roughly tick 570–580. The stripes are sharp and constant in width, with a faint diffusive halo around them and dark purple everywhere ahead of and behind the front.

These two stripes are the traveling wavefronts of the Kallen–Arcuri–Murray PDE realized on the discretized 1D lattice: each stripe traces the peak of the local outbreak as it sweeps from patch to patch via the sub/super-diagonal $D$-weighted transmission network. The constant slopes are the symmetric left- and right-going wavespeeds. **The figure demonstrates that the LASER SIR model with a tridiagonal-$D$ transmission matrix reproduces the qualitative behavior of the rabies diffusion PDE: a single seeded patch launches two symmetric, constant-velocity traveling waves of infection across the 1D lattice.**
