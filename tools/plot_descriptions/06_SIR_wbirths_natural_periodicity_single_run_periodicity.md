### Reading the single-run periodicity plot

A blue time series of mean-subtracted, Gaussian-smoothed (sigma=50 days) infected count $I_t$ against tick index, showing the post-`cutoff` window (10-year transient dropped) of a 30-year simulation at $\beta = 0.4$, `inf_mean` $= 12$, CBR $= 90$. Red `x` markers pick out local peaks and black `x` markers pick out local valleys (each constrained to be at least $T_{\text{exp}}/2$ apart). Top-right text panel reports `T expected` (analytic $T \approx 2\pi\sqrt{A G}$) alongside three observed periods — one from peak-finding, one from valley-finding, one from an FFT-based autocorrelation. The analytic value is approximately 2.0 years; the three observed estimators typically land within a few percent of it.

This is the intrinsic damped oscillation of the SIR-with-births system around its endemic equilibrium — the system is a stochastic non-linear oscillator with natural period $T \approx 2\pi\sqrt{A G}$, and the three independent estimators (time-domain peaks, time-domain valleys, frequency-domain autocorrelation FFT) all agree with the analytic prediction to within a few percent. **The figure demonstrates that the linearized analytic period $T \approx 2\pi\sqrt{A G}$ correctly predicts the natural oscillation period of the stochastic SIR model with vital dynamics on a single realization.** A full-fidelity 100-year version of this notebook (10-simulation sweep, 6-CBR regime robustness) is kept at `docs/tutorials/notebooks/research/06_SIR_wbirths_natural_periodicity.ipynb` for research use.
